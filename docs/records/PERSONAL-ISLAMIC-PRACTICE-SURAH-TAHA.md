## Purpose

This record captures a personal intention: to keep learning and practising Islamic values while working, building technology, and living everyday life.

The purpose is not to turn religion into a product, an AI feature, a public teaching claim, or a performance metric. It is a private commitment expressed in a versioned note: remember Allah, examine one's intentions, and try to let what is learned influence conduct and decisions.

## Surah Taha as a reflection reference

Surah Taha (20) is the initial Qur'anic reference named for this learning context.

Two passages that may serve as reflection anchors are:

- **Taha 20:14** — a reminder connected with remembering Allah and establishing prayer.
- **Taha 20:25–28** — the supplication of Prophet Musa (peace be upon him), asking for his heart to be opened and his task to be made easier.

These references are starting points for personal reading and reflection, not an exhaustive summary or interpretation of the surah. For exact Arabic text, translation, and tafsir, consult a trusted Qur'an edition and qualified sources.

## How this intention relates to everyday work

While working on AMRHZ, AP1, learning, problem-solving, or ordinary responsibilities, the personal aim is to:

1. Renew and examine intention rather than assume that every activity is automatically an act of worship.
2. Remember Allah and remain attentive to prayer and religious obligations.
3. Practise honesty, trustworthiness, patience, humility, and fairness in decisions and interactions.
4. Avoid allowing technical ambition, productivity, or recognition to displace religious responsibilities.
5. Treat mistakes as opportunities for correction and accountability.
6. Keep learning from reliable Islamic sources instead of treating an AI system or this note as religious authority.

## A simple personal reflection loop

- **Before:** Pause, remember Allah, and examine the intention.
- **During:** Work honestly, patiently, and responsibly; do not neglect obligations.
- **After:** Reflect on conduct, acknowledge mistakes, make corrections, and seek to improve.

This is a flexible personal reminder, not a prescribed religious ritual or a replacement for established acts of worship.
